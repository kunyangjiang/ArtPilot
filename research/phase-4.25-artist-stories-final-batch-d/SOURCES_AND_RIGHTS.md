## Story 15 — Xu Wei

**Engine.** His own ranking — 书一、诗二、文三、画四 — against how later viewers remember him; why a man ranked his
painting last, and what the paper says back.

**Sources.** NPM open data (ids 3357, 5633, 5632, 3608, 11131, 2846: descriptions, inscriptions, seals, the 1570
“醉后呼管” colophon); Palace Museum, Beijing (*Ink Grapes*, dpm 231318, link only); 徐渭《自为墓志铭》; 《明史》卷288;
袁宏道《徐文长传》; Shih Shou-chien 2023; Britannica.

**Images.** NPM IIIF, CC BY 4.0 (credit “品名。国立故宫博物院，台北，CC BY 4.0 @ www.npm.gov.tw”): the pomegranate scroll
(full + fruit crop), 花竹, 螃蟹, the calligraphy essay, rain bamboo, and the long 写生 handscroll for `ScrollUnroll`.

**Cautions kept.** The homicide and the suicide attempts are stated from the sources without drama; no modern
diagnosis; a Band says the suffering was not the condition of his art. The year he left prison (sources differ) and
whether the 1570 colophon was written in prison are stated as open.

## Story 16 — Bada Shanren

**Engine.** The upturned eye as a recurring mark, followed through names and signatures (the `NameLedger`) rather
than read as a political code in every bird.

**Sources.** The Met 1989.363.135 (*Birds and Lotus Pond*, satin; inscriptions tab), 1989.363.131 (*Two Eagles*),
the letter to Fang Shiguan, the 1699 landscape, the *Lanting* copy (Met 49144); NPM 1659 album 清傳綮寫生冊 (ids 4734–
4748: the 己亥七月 inscription “灌园长老”, the watermelon poems); Mae Anna Pang (cited as Pang).

**Images.** The Met Open Access (CC0) and NPM (CC BY 4.0). The Met *Fish and Rocks* belongs to course 39 and is
not used.

**Cautions kept.** A Callout that not every bird is political; the 驴 note glossed; unverified claims (奉新, the
“Zhang Daqian forged Bada” generalization) removed.

## Story 17 — Pan Yuliang

**Engine.** Her names — 陈、张、潘、世秀、玉良、潘张玉良 — as the record of a self-made professional identity.

**Sources.** 姚玳玫, 《守护自我：潘玉良与她的画作（1928—1937）》, CAFA Art Museum; China Museum Association chronology
(2017); 北京青年报 / chinawriter (2025) for the headstone year; Paris Musées (Cernuschi) M.C. 9700, 12358, 12359,
12361, 9650.3, 11891.

**Rights.** Pan died 1977: protected in China to end-2027 and in France to end-2047. No reproduction; official
pages with guided observation. The one image is a site-drawn diagram of two drawing methods (geometric solids; no
work's image or shape).

**Cautions kept.** The fictionalized early life is not retold; its 1983 public correction is recorded. `SortGame`
separates works recorded as self-portraits from works not so recorded. The story is not framed as Artemisia's.

## Story 18 — Michelangelo

**Engine.** Commissions, labour and negotiation: the forty-statue tomb contract of 1505, followed through what was
promised, quarried, delegated, changed and left unfinished; it ends at the Accademia *Atlas*.

**Sources.** Galleria dell'Accademia, *Atlas* (1525–30, h. 277 cm, Inv. Scult. 1080; tomb, Cosimo I, Boboli grotto,
1909; chisel, rasp and bow-drill traces) and *David* (16 Aug 1501, 25 Jan 1504 committee, 8 Sep 1504, 517 cm,
5,560 kg, gilding, 1873); Britannica biography and the Sistine Q&A (scaffold, standing; assistants from hundreds of
sketches; reduced transfer); British Museum 1859,0625.567 (first ceiling design; link only); Vasari 1568 and Condivi
1553, marked as such wherever used.

**Images.** Wikimedia Commons: *Atlas* (Jörg Bittner Unna, CC BY-SA 3.0); *Pietà* (Stanislav Traykov, CC BY 2.5);
*David* (Livioandronico2013, CC BY-SA 4.0); Sistine ceiling (Qypchak, CC BY-SA 3.0, colour-adjusted by uploader —
noted); Laurentian vestibule (Richard Mortel, CC BY 2.0); Boijmans arm study for *Drunkenness of Noah* (PD-Art).
The BM drawing is CC BY-NC-SA and is not copied. Course 43's *Libyan Sibyl* fresco, red-chalk study and the sonnet
are referenced, not reused.

**Cautions kept.** Not “lying on his back” (`InferenceChain`); not “alone” (assistants, workshop practice; which
hand did what stays unknowable); “deliberately unfinished” marked as a later reading.

## Story 19 — Pablo Picasso

**Engine.** Faces repainted: Gertrude Stein's face painted out and redone without the sitter (1906); the two
right-hand faces of *Les Demoiselles*; the states of *Guernica* photographed by Dora Maar; the 44 *Meninas*
variations of 1957.

**Sources.** The Met 47.106 (Stein: “nearly ninety” sittings doubted; face painted out spring 1906; redone after
Gósol); Met Heilbrunn essay (Voorhies 2004); MoMA 333.1939 (*Demoiselles*: dates, size, title, sources, hundreds of
studies, colonial channels, Kahnweiler and Picasso quotes); The Met 56.48 catalogue (El Greco's *Vision of Saint
John* seen in Zuloaga's studio; “crucial role”); Museu Picasso Barcelona (*Science and Charity* MPB 110.046; *Las
Meninas* series: 58 works, Aug–Dec 1957, 1968 gift, Sabartés); Museo Reina Sofía DE00050 (*Guernica*: 1 May–4 June
1937, 349.3 × 776.6 cm, purchase, MoMA loan, 1981, iconography, no allusion to a specific event, the hospital
room); Rethinking Guernica (bombing 26 Apr 1937; commission; Dora Maar biography and the 28 photographs; Picasso's
refusal to interpret; 1968 refusal; Tony Shafrazi 28 Feb 1974).

**Rights.** Picasso died 1973 (Spain life+80 → end-2053; EU ≥ end-2043; US 1937 publication → end-2032). Typographic
card; official pages with guided observation; `SizeLineup` draws empty frames from published sizes. Dora Maar's
photographs (VEGAP) are linked, not copied. Supporting images: El Greco 56.48, Cézanne *Bathers* 1976.201.12, Goya
*Disasters of War* 60 (22.60.25(60)) — The Met Open Access CC0; Gernika ruins 1937, Bundesarchiv Bild 183-H25224,
CC BY-SA 3.0 DE. Velázquez *Las Meninas* (used by another lesson) and Gris's *Portrait of Picasso* (course 65) are
not reproduced.

**Cautions kept.** Not organized by partners; a section names Fernande Olivier, Olga Khokhlova, Dora Maar, Françoise
Gilot and Jacqueline Roque by their own work, and states that harm is not to be read as “the price of genius”
without retelling details. Bull/horse symbolism is `unknowable` in the `InferenceChain`.

## Story 20 — Käthe Kollwitz

**Engine.** Eighteen years from Peter's death (22 Oct 1914) to the installation of *Grieving Parents* (July 1932),
and before that the twenty years of social printmaking that the grief fell upon.

**Chronology verified.** Kollwitz Museum Köln, memorial page: final conception “after eighteen years of dealing with
the subject”; the discarded three-figure plan; faces of the artist and her husband; letter to Kaemmerer 11 Oct
1932; Vladslo since 1956; Cologne replica (Mataré workshop, Heerich and Beuys, +10 %, 1959). Biography page: every
dated life event in the story (1867–1945), including the 1898 medal veto, the 1906 and 1912 posters, *Simplicissimus*
1908–10, the 1915–19 Havelhöhe plan, the 1918 Dehmel letter, the 1924 concept, the 1926 visit, 1931 plaster, 1932
granite, Lu Xun 1930–36, 1933–45.

**Rights.** Kollwitz died 1945: public domain in the EU since 2016; works published before 1931 are public domain in
the US. *Grieving Parents* (1932) may remain protected in the US to end-2027 and its photographs carry
photographers' rights, so the card is typographic with the museum page. Images: SMK KKS19693 and KKS19696 (Public
Domain Mark); Rijksmuseum RP-T-1960-79 (CC0); Kupferstichkabinett Berlin impressions of *Losbruch* via Commons
(PD-old-70, a reworked proof and the 1921 edition); *Die Freiwilligen* 2nd state via Commons (PD-art,
PD-old-auto-1923). The Met *Die Eltern* (2017.230) is linked only (the Met does not release it).

**Cautions kept.** The social and political work before 1914 is told first; `SortGame` places three works before or
after 1914. The “Black Anna” model and Peter's presence in *Die Freiwilligen* are marked as common readings. Courses
64 and 74 share themes only; no work overlaps.

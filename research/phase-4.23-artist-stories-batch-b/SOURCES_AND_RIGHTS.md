## Story 06 — Su Shi

**Engine.** The handscroll read from right to left: Su's poems, Huang Tingjian's colophon, the later colophons; each
part opens a stretch of his life (Huangzhou 1082 → Huzhou and the Wutai case 1079 → Dongpo → the 1083 Red Cliff note
→ Huang's colophon c. 1100 → Huizhou, Danzhou, death 1101 → the scroll's nine centuries).

**Primary documents examined.**
- NPM open data, 北宋苏东坡书黄州寒食诗 卷 (购书 001001): transcription; dimensions; full texts of the colophons by
  Huang Tingjian, Dong Qichang, Qianlong (1748), Zhang Yan, Yan Shiqing (1918), Naitō Torajirō (1924, two), Luo
  Zhenyu (1924), Guo Yimin, Wang Shijie (1959); seals; museum descriptions (dates 1082; Huang before the ninth month
  of 1100; “字也越写越大”; long final strokes of 年、中、葦、紙).
- NPM open data, 宋苏轼书前赤壁赋 卷 (故书 000068): the closing note to Fu Yaoyu (“多难畏事……必深藏之不出也”),
  written 1083; the opening 36 characters supplied by Wen Zhengming; “现今仅存的赤壁赋手迹”.
- 《宋史·苏轼传》 (Wikisource): the charges from the memorial and poems, “欲置之死”, demotion to 黄州团练副使安置,
  “筑室于东坡，自号东坡居士”, Huizhou, Changhua (“非人所居”), “卒于常州，年六十六”.
- Su Shi, 《文与可画筼筜谷偃竹记》: Wen Tong's death (1079, first month) and 7 July 1079 in Huzhou, “废卷而哭失声”.
- Su Shi, 《自题金山画像》 (1101).

**Quotations.** All Chinese quotations are classical texts (public domain), quoted from the NPM transcriptions or the
texts above; modern-Chinese glosses are ArtPilot's.

**Images (all NPM open data, CC BY 4.0, credit “品名。国立故宫博物院，台北，CC BY 4.0 @ www.npm.gov.tw”).**
- `su-shi-cold-food-npm.jpg` (5950 × 640): NPM supplies the scroll as overlapping 6-megapixel photographs
  (B2B001001N000000000PAA–PAG). ArtPilot aligned them by normalised cross-correlation and placed them side by side
  (translation only: no warping, no colour change), cropped the colour chart and table, scaled to 640 px high. Only
  frontispiece to Dong Qichang's colophon; the later colophons are not included.
- `-opening`, `-corrections`, `-damage`, `-huang`: crops of the stitched strip.
- `su-shi-red-cliff-note-npm.jpg`: two adjacent NPM photographs (K2B000068N000000000PAJ, PAK) aligned the same way,
  cropped to the closing note.

**Interpretive limits stated in the story.** The growing size and slant of the characters is a reading, not a record
of feeling; what Huang's “李西台” refers to was debated (NPM bibliography); how the Zhang family obtained the scroll
is not recorded; whether Su ever saw Huang's colophon is unknown; the Wutai dates are given to the month only.

**Overlap.** Courses 26–27 (Northern and Southern Song painting) are referenced, not retold; no landscape painting is
used.

## Story 07 — Rembrandt

**Engine.** The 1656 inventory as a floor plan walked room by room (`InventoryRooms`), each room opening a part of his
life: Leiden and Lievens (front hall), workshop and dealing (side room), the Ruffo commission, collecting and
printmaking (art cabinet), Titus (anteroom), props (studios), then after the sale.

**Primary documents examined.**
- Montias Database (The Frick Collection), inventory 1262: Amsterdam City Archives, Desolate Boedelskamer, DBK
  5072/364, fols. 29r–38v, dated 26 July 1656; introduction and postscript (“den 25: en 26: Julij 1656”); residence;
  marriage date; all 321 entries with verbatim Dutch, type and room. Every entry quoted in the story was checked
  against this transcription (lots 0001, 0012, 0016, 0029, 0030, 0039, 0123, 0141, 0151, 0191, 0232, 0237, 0247,
  0270, 0271, 0276, 0283; 0033 and 0092 paraphrased). Counts per room and per artist were computed from the same data.
- Rembrandt House Museum (two pages): “narrowly avoided being declared bankrupt” (followed; the story explains why
  it does not say “bankruptcy”, noting the Met audio guide's different wording); inventory drawn up by a city official;
  house 1639–1658; Rozengracht; eleven more years; Titus, Hendrickje, Cornelia; the museum furnished after the list.
- Metropolitan Museum: *Aristotle with a Bust of Homer* catalogue entry (Ruffo; Messina 1654; Ruffo's 1657 inventory
  “possibly Aristotle or Albertus Magnus”; Guercino's 1660 letter; 1662 complaint); *Self-Portrait* 1660 audio
  (Liedtke and Christiansen on paint handling and the market).
- Rijksmuseum linked data: RP-P-OB-796 (Segers, printed in olive-green ink), RP-P-1962-31 (“on a Plate by Segers”),
  SK-A-3138 (Titus, possibly as St Francis).

**Translations.** Dutch inventory entries translated by ArtPilot; the transcription is factual record.

**Images (all public domain, CC0).** Met: 14.40.618, 61.198, 17.37.71, 20.46.12. Rijksmuseum: RP-P-OB-796,
RP-P-1962-31, SK-A-3138. Scaled and recompressed only. The mirrored view in `SamePlate` flips the print in the
browser and is labelled as not a photograph of the plate.

**Interpretive limits stated in the story.** Whether the painted bust is the inventory's “Homerus” (plausible, not
proven); who named the painting Aristotle; which props went into which paintings; the idea that hardship deepened his
art (argued against).

**Overlap.** Courses 45 and 47 referenced; no genre painting, no market survey; the Night Watch is not shown.

## Story 08 — Zhang Daqian

**Engine.** Three characters, “大千临”, on an openly declared copy, against the documented fact that the same painter
made forgeries; then his life from Neijiang to Taipei as a movement from copying to his own splashed-colour painting.
The story classifies objects only by their holders' records and does not resolve any attribution.

**Sources.**
- Met 2002.605a–d (description; four differences from Shitao; inscriptions and seals transcribed in the Met's
  record: “清湘老人濟 / 大千臨”, seals “大千毫髮”, “張季蝯印”, “張爰，阿蝯”).
- Met 1972.122a–l (Shitao, *Wilderness Colors*); Met 1989.363.189 (“Attributed to” Zhang); Met 2005.510.5
  (splashed ink and colour, mid-1960s; Zao Wou-ki as possible inspiration).
- Smithsonian National Museum of Asian Art provenance biography (2016): family, Zhang Shanzi, Kyoto 1917, Shanghai
  teachers, Pu Ru, Master of the Nets Garden, Dunhuang 1940 and four years of return visits, Brazil 1952, Picasso 1956
  with exchange of paintings, forgeries in the Met, MFA Boston and Freer.
- Britannica (Law and Wan): dates; Tang Yin, Chen Hongshou, Shitao; a forged Shitao that deceived connoisseurs;
  “South Zhang and North Pu”; Mogao and Yulin, more than 200 paintings; collection; Mendoza, São Paulo, Carmel; Nice;
  eye problems and splashed colour; Wang Mo; Moye Jingshe.
- NPM open data: brush 赠文 000007 (1969, Kōseidō and Gyokusendō, 50 brushes, “艺坛主盟”); *Mushroom* 购画 001198
  (link only); seal “张爰” 赠杂 000115.

**Rights.** Zhang (d. 1983) is in copyright; no work by him is reproduced. NPM's open-data page labels *Mushroom*
CC BY 4.0, but it is a purchased work and ArtPilot did not verify that NPM holds the copyright, so it is linked, not
copied. Images used: Shitao's four leaves (Met, CC0) and the 1969 brush (NPM, CC BY 4.0; cropped to remove the
inventory tag). The Duan ink pot (赠文 000033) was not used: its image is not on the open IIIF server, and the
download page requires a captcha.

**Interpretive limits stated.** Which forgery deceived whom (no verifiable primary record); what Zhang and Picasso
said (not quoted); the meeting place (Nice per Britannica; the Smithsonian ties it to his first Paris visit); the
sources of splashed colour (three accounts, not ranked). The Dunhuang damage controversy is not raised: no primary
source was examined.

**Overlap.** Courses 17, 38, 39 referenced. The future Chinese attribution Case is not pre-empted: no single object's
attribution is investigated or decided.

## Story 09 — Bodys Isek Kingelez

**Engine.** An explicit fictional frame (a visitor from an imagined future, in `Callout kind="fiction"`, at the opening
and the close), then the documented biography, then close looking at the real models through MoMA's records.

**Fictional device.** Marked in the prelude and by the “虚构 · 不是史实” label each time. The visitor is not a witness;
the city he sees is described only with features visible in MoMA's photograph of *Kimbembele Ihunga* (tower,
fan-shaped stadium, curved track, patterned base) and materials in MoMA's list (lights, foil). It does not claim
prediction, construction plans or a utopian outcome.

**Sources.** MoMA artist page (Hillary Reder, 2018, with its notes citing Soulillou & Girard 1984, Kingelez's
statements in *Perspectives 145*, 2005, and Dirk Dumon's 2004 film); MoMA *City Dreams* exhibition page; object
records 222150 (*Kimbembele Ihunga*, PG130.2019, full materials, 130 × 185 × 320 cm, kids label), 222868 (*U.N.*,
131.2019), 223287 (*Kinshasa la Belle*, 128.2019). Quotations are short translations by ArtPilot of statements MoMA
publishes.

**Rights.** Kingelez (d. 2015) is in copyright; no image of his work is reproduced or imitated. The opening image
`kingelez-scale-artpilot.jpg` is an ArtPilot scale diagram: a plain rectangle of the published outer measurements
beside a 170 cm figure and a 200 cm door. `SizeLineup` draws only outlines.

**Overlap.** Courses 82 and 84 referenced, not retold.

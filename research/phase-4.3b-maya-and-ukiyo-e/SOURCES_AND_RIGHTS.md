Claims and their sources:

- Lintel placement (horizontal over a temple doorway, carved face down, viewed looking up, perhaps by torchlight);
  La Pasadita between Yaxchilan and Piedras Negras; Tiloom (sajal) on lintels of 759 (Berlin), 766 (Leiden), 771
  (private collection); the Met's interpretation of the 771 lintel; Chel Te' Chan K'inich = Shield Jaguar IV
  (pre-accession name); the squeezed caption "almost as if … not originally planned"; sculptor signatures around
  Yaxchilan and Piedras Negras; at least a dozen related lintels from subsidiary sites; Shield Jaguar IV sending
  sculptors to provincial lords including Bonampak; site access only from 1998: Met object page 313240 (James Doyle,
  2015).
- Inscription readings ("Chel Te' Chan K'inich, holy lord of Yaxchilan; it is his sculpture, Chakalte'" / "Tiloom,
  sahal"): Met object page, "Signatures, Inscriptions, and Markings". ArtPilot does not translate glyphs.
- Classic city-states, court sculptors, signatures, wooden lintels: Met essay *Ancient Maya Sculpture* (Doyle, 2016).
- Puuc column (possible ruler, no text; War Serpent headdress from Teotihuacan; ancestor heads; paired column in
  Worcester; flanked a doorway): Met object page 310540.
- Yaxchilan Structure 33 lintels in situ; Copán Stela A (731; four great kingdoms); Copán–Quiriguá (724/725
  installation, 734 "holy lord", 738 capture and execution): Wikipedia "Yaxchilan", "Copán", "Quiriguá", which cite
  Martin & Grube, *Chronicle of the Maya Kings and Queens*, and Looper, *Lightning Warrior* (2003). **The books
  themselves were not consulted** (stated in the lesson's sources).
- Bonampak: one sentence only; no mural or warfare analysis (reserved for the Bonampak Case).

Claims and their sources:

- 1765 nishiki-e; first polychrome prints were calendars commissioned by wealthy Edo patrons; actors and courtesans,
  later vistas; "appealed in particular to the wealthy townspeople"; designer, engraver, printer and publisher; the
  publisher chose theme and quality; design transferred to thin paper, pasted on a (usually cherry) block; up to about
  twenty blocks; two registration cuts at the block edge; mulberry paper; impressions "sometimes numbering in the
  thousands": Met essay *Woodblock Prints in the Ukiyo-e Style* (2003).
- Harunobu calendar (months in the kimono pattern, uninked embossing; commissioner's seal; carver Sekine Kaei): Met
  object page JP2438.
- Women performing the workshop roles in Utamaro's triptych: directly visible; the reading "not a documentary record" is
  stated as interpretation.
- Not claimed: any price; who or how many bought prints; which Hiroshige impression is earlier; the 28-portrait set
  (seen only in secondary sources).

## Educational reconstructions and overlays (labelled on the page)

- `LintelView`: original placement vs museum display; schematic, after the Met's account.
- `WoodblockProcess`: four steps after the Met essay; block numbers and shapes are schematic.
- `RegistrationGame`: a simplified design printed block by block; not a reconstruction of any print.
- `TorchView`: light, angle and darkness are a simulation for looking, not a reconstruction of site lighting.
- Marks on photographs and the targets of the "find" games are placed by eye and labelled as teaching annotations.

- Piece-mold casting; decoration carved/stamped on the mold's inner face; piece-mold probably the only method until at
  least the end of Shang; components of the taotie (eyes, nose, jaws, horns, split body); its significance "currently …

- Fu Hao tomb (Yinxu): excavated 1976, unlooted, 468 bronzes, about 109 inscribed 妇好: 中国社会科学网 (2023),
  https://www.cssn.cn/kgxc/kgxc_kgxl/202304/t20230420_5623450.shtml. The excavation report (《殷墟妇好墓》, 文物出版社)
  was not consulted directly.
- Interpretations (cited as interpretations): K. C. Chang 1983; Max Loehr 1953 / Robert Bagley 1987; specific-animal
  readings. Flanges and mold joins: stated as an interpretation drawn from many objects, not a rule.
- Not claimed: which Song work first applied the name 饕餮 (not established in this research).

Claims and their sources:

- Roman copying (plaster casts, measuring points, struts often carved as tree trunks; most bronze originals lost; copies
  as primary visual evidence): Met, *Roman Copies of Greek Statues* (2002),
  https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/roman-copies-of-greek-statues
- Diadoumenos fame (mentioned three times in ancient literature; over 25 full-size marble copies), Giustiniani history,
  removed restorations: Met object page 246991.
- Kouros: among the earliest marble figures from Attica; stance derived from Egyptian art; stood over a young Athenian
  aristocrat's grave: Met object page 253370.
- Pliny, *Natural History* 34.55 (Latin checked on LacusCurtius): Diadumenos "molliter iuvenem, centum talentis
  nobilitatum"; Doryphoros "viriliter puerum"; the statue artists call "canona".
- Galen, *De placitis Hippocratis et Platonis* 5.3 (quoting Chrysippus on the commensurability of parts): given in
  Chinese as a paraphrase from published English translations; see Papini, *Lexicon Philosophicum* 2018; Pollitt 1995.
- Not claimed: any numeric formula of the Canon; that the "canon" statue was the Doryphoros (stated as disputed); the
  original bronze's surface or inlays; that the ideal body stood for all Greeks.

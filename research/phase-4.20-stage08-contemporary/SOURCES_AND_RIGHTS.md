# Phase 4.20 — Stage 08, courses 84–90: sources and rights

Verified 2026-10-29. Courses 79–83 are deliberately unpublished (deferred to a later phase), so Stage 08 is published
out of order: 74–78 (Phase 4.19) and 84–90 (this phase). Every course awards one card and none awards extras, so each
fills its reserved slot and no existing card number changes: the new cards are No.120–126, Pollock stays No.110,
Richter No.114, and The Tortoise Trainer No.127.

Rights policy applied (unchanged from Phase 4.19, see `research/phase-4.19-stage08-batch-a/SOURCES_AND_RIGHTS.md`):

- A principal work that cannot lawfully be reproduced has no image of itself on the site. Its record carries an
  `officialPage` checked in a browser to show that very work, and its card is a first-class text-only card.
- Supporting images are other works or documents, each named by its `subject`, never shown as the principal work.
- Public-domain status is decided from documented publication history and the maker's death date (US: published
  before 1931, or a US government document; Germany/EU: 70 years after death), not from a Commons tag alone.
- A museum's or photographer's permission never covers the underlying artwork.
- Nothing was purchased or requested from a rights holder.

Why these works (editorial requirements of the Phase 4.20 authorization):

- **84 — immersion, light, perception, movement, the viewer.** *The weather project* is the most documented immersive
  installation of its decade, and its documentation records what visitors did (lying down, waving, spelling words), so
  the lesson can separate what the artist built from what the audience brought. Its precedent, the painted panorama, is
  documented in a public-domain 1801 plate and a still-standing 1881 panorama.
- **85 — moving image, animation, sound, rhythm, duration, multi-screen.** *The Refusal of Time* combines five
  projections, a 30-minute duration, a spatial soundtrack and a kinetic sculpture, and its maker's charcoal-erasure
  animation is a teachable technique. Ruttmann's 1921 abstract film introduces rhythm in moving images; it is
  described and linked, not shown (see the film rights check under Course 85).
- **86 — generative systems, algorithms, data, AI, authorship.** The V&A sheet records a precise division of labour
  (program-drawn lines 1977, the artist's hand colour and signature 1982); with Jacquard's loom and Lovelace's 1843 notes
  it carries the authorship question from punched cards to today's image generators.
- **87 — games, player agency, rules, playable art.** *Passage* is in MoMA's first group of collected video games and
  its maker placed it in the public domain, so it can be shown, not only linked. Magie's 1904 patent shows rules as
  argument.
- **88 — wearable sculpture, experimental fashion, materials, movement.** The 1997 collection changed the body's
  outline with padding and was danced in Cunningham's *Scenario*; Fuller's 1894 patent and Schlemmer's 1925 figurine
  chart (both public domain) give the history of clothing that makes or shapes movement.
- **89 — internet art, websites, participation, interface, networked experience.** Lialina's 1996 work still runs at
  its original address, is built from HTML frames whose behaviour can be taught, and has a documented history of remakes;
  Douglas Davis's *World's First Collaborative Sentence* (Whitney) adds participation and conservation.
- **90 — contemporary painting, close looking, materiality, conclusion.** Hockney's 50-canvas painting is about how a
  painter sees something bigger than one canvas, uses photographs and a computer only as tools, and was painted
  outdoors; Rand's 1841 paint-tube patent ties materials to plein-air painting (course 60). Hockney died on
  11 June 2026; Tate's record already reads "1937–2026".

Repeats checked against every existing lesson and record (`grep` of names and image paths): none of these works, and no
supporting image, appears in another lesson. Van Gogh's *Cypresses* (Met) was considered for course 90 and rejected as
too close to the existing *Wheat Field with Cypresses* (course 61).

#### Final targeted film rights check (Ruttmann, *Lichtspiel Opus 1*) — 2026-10-29: excerpt removed

The first draft played a 90-second silent excerpt and showed one still. The check below separated the 1921 film from
the copy actually used.

1. **Exact source file.** Wikimedia Commons, [File:Lichtspiel Opus I (1921).webm](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lichtspiel_Opus_I_(1921).webm),
   current version uploaded 2013-12-29 17:00 UTC by User:Racconish with the comment "rm audio" (480 × 360, 11:43,
   11,912,870 bytes, SHA-1 `1f9b8009216aed2bd6edb173c54f24ce2265b75d`; the downloaded file's SHA-1 matches). It
   replaced a first version uploaded 20 minutes earlier with sound (21.4 MB).
2. **Uploader, archive, distributor, terms.** The Commons page names no archive, restorer or distributor. Its only
   source is a YouTube video (`FYJnZ946L1c`, in a playlist); YouTube now returns "Not Found" for it, so its uploader and
   terms cannot be checked. The only licence given is Commons' `{{PD-old-70-1923}}` tag for the underlying film. The
   copy ends with credits of a modern presentation (music Max Butting; musical direction Titus Engel; five named
   string players), i.e. a modern restoration or digital master with a modern recording, source unidentified.
   Several restorations exist (e.g. Il Cinema Ritrovato lists a 2008 edition with Butting's score restored by Timothy
   Brock; Light Cone distributes a digital copy "prior to the 1981 restoration" for rental at €52). Silent Era's
   database lists the Friedrich-Wilhelm-Murnau-Stiftung as current rights holder.
3. **Underlying film.** Public domain in the US (released 1921, before 1931) and in Germany/EU (Ruttmann died 1941;
   70 years p.m.a. ended 31 Dec 2011). Butting's 1921 score is not: Butting died in 1976.
4. **The copy used.** Not cleared. Its restoration and digital master cannot be identified; German law grants film
   producers' and moving-picture rights (UrhG §§ 94, 95) to new recordings of films that a restoration or new master
   may attract, and the EU rule for reproductions of public-domain works (DSM Art. 14, UrhG § 68) is not settled for
   film restorations. The audio track was removed by the uploader, but the picture itself comes from the same modern
   master; removing the end credits removes no rights.
5. **Editing, local hosting, worldwide streaming.** Not adequately supported, since the source cannot be traced to an
   archive or a licence.

**Action:** the local clip (`public/media/ruttmann-opus1-clip.webm`, `.mp4`), the still
(`public/img/ruttmann-opus1-poster.jpg`) and their homepage-table copies were deleted. Course 85 keeps its account
of the film (from filmportal.de) and now gives an observation guide plus two verified links: the filmportal.de film
record and Light Cone's distribution page. The film is described, not shown.

Facts: media, duration, ownership, the Galison/Einstein/Poincaré account, the 1870s Paris pneumatic tubes and the
Dickens elephant — The Met; commission for dOCUMENTA (13) by Carolyn Christov-Bakargiev, collaborators (Galison,
Meyburgh, Miller, Masilo and others), five projections, megaphones with different soundtracks — SFMOMA press release
(16/17 Oct 2013); erase-and-rephotograph method and *Mine* (1991) — Tate Papers 14; accumulated traces — MoMA
*Projects 68* catalogue (1998); Ruttmann's premieres, music, 10,000 coloured frames, self-built device —
filmportal.de. The Dickens sentence (*Hard Times*, 1854, Book 1 ch. 5) is public domain.

## New site capabilities (small)

- `FilmClip.astro` — plays a lawful clip in the page (no autoplay, muted, two formats), credited from the record's
  still. Not used by any lesson since the Ruttmann excerpt was removed; kept for a future cleared clip.

  and diagrams no longer read "摄影 …". Records without it render exactly as before.
- `scripts/table-images.mjs` writes PNG sources as PNG (the Passage screenshot), so a copy's format matches its name.

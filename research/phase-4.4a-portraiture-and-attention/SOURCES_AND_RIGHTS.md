Claims and sources:

- Hatshepsut declared herself king between years 2 and 7 of Thutmose III; throne name Maatkare in cartouches; nemes
  and shendyt as royal dress; "distinctly feminine air, unlike most representations of Hatshepsut as ruler"; feminized
  titles on the throne; blue/yellow pigment on the nemes; feminine seated statues probably received offerings in less
  public areas (the Met's suggestion, presented as interpretation); Dorothea Arnold's audio-guide remark: Met 29.3.2.
- At least ten over-life-size kneeling statues; male king with kilt, false beard, white crown or nemes; nu-pots; base
  inscription naming the offering to Amun; processional way of a yearly festival: Met 30.3.1.
- Huang portrait: unsigned portraitist, landscape and inscription by Ding Shangyong, shading likely reflecting
  photography (introduced to China in the 1860s): Met 2023.300. The inscription is read from the image (花農觀察大人玉照 /
  潞河少石丁尚庸補景并署款於梧雲山館). **The sitter's Chinese name is not given by the Met and was not found; the lesson uses
  the romanization only.**
- Chrysanthemum and Tao Yuanming: Tao's 《飲酒》 lines as collected in the Song 《百菊集譜》卷四 (Wikisource). The reading
  of the chrysanthemum in this portrait is stated as interpretation.
- Lavoisier portrait: Met overview (Chinese text) for Lavoisier's work and Marie Anne's illustrations and drawings;
  Technical Notes by Dorothy Mahon (2021) for the 2019 IRR and MA-XRF findings (hat, brown suit with gold buttons,
  writing desk, globe, bookshelf; later red velvet cloth and instruments). The Burlington Magazine and Heritage Science
  articles are cited through the Met record, **not read directly**.

## Course 03 — 画家是怎样引导你的目光的？ (card No.003)

Claims and sources:

- Late Naples works rely on brightly lit areas against nearly black backgrounds; the soldier's finger and the woman's
  two fingers allude to three accusations and three denials (presented as the Met's interpretation); Peter before a
  fireplace; condensation into three half-length figures (Keith Christiansen 2017): Met 1997.167. The soldier's finger
  is not marked on the image because it cannot be located reliably in the reproduction.
- Padshahnama folio: dynamism of the combat balancing the impassive royal portraits; upper courtiers face the royals,
  lower figures watch the combat; none look directly at their object; the emperor under the gilded roof as lord of all
  beneath him; Persian inscriptions naming Bulaqi son of Hushang: Met 1989.135.
- Yarbus: *Eye Movements and Vision* (1967; Russian 1965); task-dependent viewing of Repin's painting.
- Tatler, Wade, Kwan, Findlay & Velichkovsky, "Yarbus, eye movements, and vision", *i-Perception* 1(1): 7–27 (2010),
  doi:10.1068/i0382 (PMC3563050): review and replication of the instruction experiment, using a portrait of Yarbus.
- Massaro et al., "When Art Moves the Eyes", *PLoS ONE* 7(5): e37285 (2012) (PMC3356266): 42 participants without art
  training; human-figure content guided gaze more than low-level features; low-level features mattered more for nature
  scenes. Limits (lab, screens, set tasks, small samples) are stated in the lesson.

## Educational reconstructions and overlays

- Marks on photographs and the targets of the "find" games are placed by eye and labelled as teaching annotations.
- `CoverCompare` patches are ArtPilot overlays for comparison; the text about where attention goes when a part is
  covered is labelled as a plausible hypothesis, not a finding.
- `FirstLook` records nothing; it only shows the reader's own mark.

Claims and their status:

- **Met chronologies "Sudan, 2000–1000 B.C." and "Sudan, 1000 B.C.–1 A.D.":** Kerma as rival and trading partner;
  Dyn. 12 forts against Kerma; Classic Kerma royal burials under mounds with beds inlaid with ivory and bronze;
  Kerma–Hyksos alliance; New Kingdom control to the 4th cataract by ~1450 BCE; Napata, Amun; Alara, Kashta, Piye,
  Shabaqo, Taharqo; pyramids near Napata for 500+ years; move to Meroë ~250 BCE; Kushite artists "adapt Egyptian forms
  … to their own purposes" (paraphrased).
- **MFA Boston (Taharqa shawabty records):** 1917 Harvard–Boston expedition; 1,070 stone shawabtys in rows along the
  burial-chamber walls; materials vary; Nubian shawabtys known only from royal tombs.
- **Kerma accompanying burials:** Reisner 1923; "over 300" in KX per British Museum caption; the "sacrificed wives"
  reading is contested by later bioarchaeology. The lesson gives no exact number ("数以百计").
- **Ordinary burials:** J. Richards 2005, cited as a study, not quoted.
- **Interpretation, labelled:** Isis/Nephthys reading of the estate figures (Met); pyramid revival as a claim to
  ancient Egyptian legitimacy; the word "Egyptianised" questioned.
- **Shabti spell:** Book of the Dead ch. 6 (Faulkner, trans.), paraphrased.

Claims and their status:

- **Wikipedia "Olmec colossal heads"** (citing Diehl, Cyphers, Stirling): 17 heads at four sites; heights 1.17–3.4 m;
  San Lorenzo heads buried by 900 BCE; Heads 2 and 7 recarved from thrones; portrait reading.
- **Met 1974.122 label (Ikehara-Tsukayama 2024):** post-fire paint as innovation; designs derived from Cupisnique and
  Chavín; the Oculate Being specific to Ica, likely antecedent of Nasca flying figures.
- **Interpretation, labelled:** portraits of rulers; transport method; Lanzón as principal deity or oracle;
  transformation sequence of the tenon heads and the San Pedro cactus (Burger 1992); independent origin of
  Olmec–Chavín similarities.
- **Avoided:** a stone-transport distance (sources give from tens of km to over 150 km by route); the claim that only
  one tenon head remains on the wall; any Maya material (course 24).
- **Name clash:** Xalapa (Mexico) and Harappa share the usual Chinese transliteration 哈拉帕; the record and caption
  add "Xalapa" to keep them apart.

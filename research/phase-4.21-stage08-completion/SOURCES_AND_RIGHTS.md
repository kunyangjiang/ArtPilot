# Phase 4.21 — Stage 08 completion, courses 79–83: sources and rights

Verified 2026-10-29. With these five lessons all 90 core courses are published. The rights policy is the one used in
Phases 4.19–4.20: a principal work that cannot lawfully be reproduced is shown by its verified official page and a
text-only card; supporting images are other works or documents, named by `subject`, never presented as the work; public
domain is decided from publication and the maker's dates, never a tag alone; nothing was purchased or requested.

## Search, and why most principal works are text-only

- **Commons permission tickets (VRT):** scanned for 26 candidate artists. Only four files have a rights-holder release,
  and none shows a usable principal work (a Xu Bing portrait, a Shonibare decorative installation, two portraits of
  Salcedo and Vicuña).
- **Korean public licence portal (공유마당):** no usable result for Park Seo-Bo, Lee Ufan, Yun Hyong-keun, Chung
  Sang-hwa, Ha Chong-hyun, Lee Bul, Do Ho Suh, Kimsooja or Lee Seung-ja.
- **Museum open-access APIs:** the Met, Cleveland, AIC and Tate list every candidate work as in copyright (e.g.
  Cleveland `share_license_status: Copyrighted` for Park Seo-Bo; Tate `masterImageCC` empty).
- **Not used:** Commons freedom-of-panorama photographs of contemporary public works. Photographers cannot license the
  underlying artwork, and the US has no panorama exception for sculpture.

The principal works therefore use verified official pages (four of them from Tate, Cleveland, M+ and the Walker, plus
the Met). The visual teaching comes from genuine, related, open-licence works and documents (the Kangxi Dictionary
page, the Asante kente, the Inka khipu, the Harriet Powers quilt, Fragonard's *Swing*), process diagrams drawn from the
museums' descriptions, and interactions. One collectible card is image-based (the Powers quilt, CC0).

Each official page was opened in a browser (or, for Tate, read through Tate's own collection API) and shows that work.

**Why the A+ cards.** The course titles of 80 ("different paths") and 81 ("two works") require two equal case studies;
79 is built on the contrast between Xu Bing's and Huang Yong Ping's opposite treatments of the book. Each second work
is analysed at length, so it gets its own card. The Powers quilt is a major work in its own right and the lesson's
central evidence for quilt narrative; it is also the batch's only lawful image card. Course 82's supporting works (the
kente, Shonibare) were not given cards.

**Numbering.** The five reserved slots fill as before. The four A+ cards move every later display number by up to four:
course 84–90 cards are now No.124–130 (were No.120–126), and The Tortoise Trainer is No.131 (was No.127). Artwork IDs,
stored claims and timestamps are untouched (numbers are display-only). The collection now has 131 cards.

## Interactions (drawn by ArtPilot; none reproduces a work)

- `FakeCharacters` (79): combines common components; a table of 26 common characters says which pairs are real; fills a
  page with nine columns of up to 17 characters (Princeton's description of the layout). These are not Xu Bing's
  characters.
- `SurfaceProcess` (80): pierce a layered sheet; incise wet paint with a pencil.
- `SeriesWall` (81): 40 empty frames hung as a full set, groups, or a single print, then shuffled (Tate: no fixed order).
- `LinkedUnits` (82): one sheet of small units hung flat, creased, draped or gathered (the "non-fixed form").
- `KnotNumber` (83): the decimal knot code scholars have read in khipus (general history: L. L. Locke and others).

## Key facts and their sources

- **79:**
  - M+: the 4,000+ characters, Kangxi rules, Song typeface, movable-type process, 120 four-volume sets, walnut boxes,
    the 1980s reading.
  - Princeton: the uniqueness check, hand-carving, racks, 9 columns × 17, six-hole binding, the 1988 debut and the 1989
    *China/Avant-Garde*.
  - Fei Dawei (Walker Reader, 2005): Xiamen Dada (1986, show 8 Sept–5 Oct, burning 24 Nov), the wash on 1 Dec 1987,
    the two books, the "Chan answer" reading, Paris 1989.
  - MoMA post: 5 Feb 1989 shots.
  - Not claimed: installation layouts with hanging scrolls (no source found), the reconstruction place for Huang's work.
- **80:**
  - Tate T07898 (Bottinelli, 2002): materials and process, the newspapers and their cause, 1949/50, Gutai founding and
    name, the 1956 cannon, the artist's quotations, the Fontana episode.
  - Tate T07897: the foil-wrapper *Holes* and the "castrate" remark.
  - Cleveland: *Ecriture* process, erasure, the son's grid, meditation, Dansaekhwa.
  - Met: Lee Ufan, Mono-ha, chisel cuts, Newman's *Concord*.
  - Readings of Dansaekhwa as resistance are flagged as debated.
- **81:**
  - Tate T14118 and "Who is Sheela Gowda?": materials, scale, display, temple hair and the bumper talisman, Venice
    Arsenale, quotations from Trevor Smith (ed.) 2007 and *Afterall* 22.
  - Tate P13507: 40 photographs, Port Dickson, edition, hang, no order.
  - Ilham Gallery: the settings, the fruit and categorisation, friends wearing the fruit, the question of "indigenous".
  - DAAO: the sitters are family, friends and relations. The two sources differ slightly; the lesson gives both.
- **82:**
  - Met 2007.96: kente, Akan and Ewe, drape, colours, the artist's audio quotations.
  - Tate 2023 guide: the making, assistants, Nigerian bottle tops, the trade-history reading (presented as Tate's

  - Tate T14331: the "transformation" quotation.
  - Tate T07952: description of Shonibare's work.
  - Dutch wax history: general.
  - Removed as unverified: "cut and folded" caps.
- **83:**
  - Met 1990.237a-c: everything about the quilt and Lowery Stokes Sims.
  - Smithsonian: everything about Powers and her quilt.
  - Tate T15921: Vicuña's label.
  - Cleveland: khipu data.

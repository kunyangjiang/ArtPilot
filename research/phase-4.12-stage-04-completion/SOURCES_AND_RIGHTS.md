# Phase 4.12 — Stage 04 completion (Courses 32–37): sources, image rights and selection

Checked 2026-10-28. Museum data from the institutions' own object pages and open-access APIs (Cleveland
`openaccess-api.clevelandart.org`; Walters, British Museum, Kimbell and ColBase pages read in the built-in browser,
the BM curator's comment opened with its "View more" control). UNESCO World Heritage pages read in the built-in browser
(the site refuses non-browser clients). Official site texts from the Patronato de la Alhambra y Generalife (English and
Spanish building pages). Chinese primary texts from zh.wikisource (raw wikitext). Commons licence data from the Commons
API and the raw file descriptions; files downloaded with `Special:FilePath?width=1920` (a standard thumbnail size; Commons
returned 429 on larger requests). Every image was resized to at most 1600 px on its long side unless noted, recompressed
and, for museum files, kept its colour profile. Nothing was recoloured, sharpened or annotated in the file. CC BY-SA
images: the resized files are offered under the same licence.

Not used: the British Museum's own images (CC BY-NC-SA); a 1977 book scan of the Ife head on Commons (its public-domain
claim is doubtful); a Brihadishvara photo carrying another photographer's watermark; a Bagan mural of uncertain date;
a Commons plan photograph that reproduces a modern signboard.

## Course 32 — Alhambra, Qusayr ʿAmra, Isfahan (`islamic-palaces-and-squares.mdx`, card No.032)

- **Patronato de la Alhambra y Generalife:** Comares façade (Muhammad V, 1370, Algeciras; audience and justice at the
  top of the stairs; right door to family and servants, left door to the core by a dark Z-shaped corridor); Court of the
  Myrtles (houses vs palaces, wealth shown in the courtyard); Salón de los Embajadores (45 m tower, nine alcoves, central
  one for the Sultan, ceiling's symbolic programme; two inscriptions translated from the Patronato's Spanish); Court of
  the Lions (Muhammad V 1362–91; fountain hydraulics; open debate on paved vs planted quarters); Sala de dos Hermanas
  (muqarnas dome); Mexuar (council and justice; possibly Ismāʿīl I; 16th-c chapel; moved epigraphic frieze; 1590
  explosion, 1917 restoration); Palace of Charles V (1526; symbolism; altered structure and access; 1637; 1923).
- **UNESCO 314:** palatine city; water; criterion (iii) plaster, wood, ceramics, “talking architecture”.
- **Met Heilbrunn, “The Art of the Nasrid Period”:** dynasty dates, vassalage from 1243, “red castle”, royal city, the
  “stage set” characterisation (paraphrased and labelled as interpretation), 1492.
- **UNESCO 327 and World Monuments Fund:** Qusayr ʿAmra's date, plan, frescoes, zodiac dome; the 2012 inscription naming
  Walid ibn Yazid (723–743); the six-kings identifications (WMF's wording); removal of 1970s shellac.
- **UNESCO 115:** Meidan Emam's size, buildings, uses, Ali Qapu and the throne room.
- **Plan:** Uhde 1892, coloured by a Commons editor; its legend (Comares / Lions / Mexuar / Emperor's chambers) is
  used; its unexplained numbers are not interpreted; compass directions are not claimed.
- **Images:** Commons — Tuxyso (CC BY-SA 3.0), Palickap (CC BY-SA 4.0), J. L. Filpo Cabana (CC BY 4.0 / CC BY-SA 4.0),
  Benjamin Smith (CC BY-SA 4.0), Sharon Mollerus (CC BY 2.0), R Prazeres (CC BY-SA 4.0), Amir Pashaei (CC BY-SA 4.0),
  Uhde plan (public domain).

## Course 33 — Chola bronze and temple (`temple-bronze-ritual.mdx`, card No.033)

- **Cleveland 1930.331:** dates, size, iconographic reading, provenance (C. T. Loo → CMA 1930; earlier history blank).
  1961.94 (posts once held a halo), 1971.117 (base photo).
- **Walters 54.2850:** procession (poles through base rings into chariot or palanquin, lowered for offerings), lost-wax
  steps, solid casting, continuing tradition, provenance (Rockefeller III; C. T. Loo; Sotheby's 1993).
- **UNESCO 250:** Rajaraja I, 1003–04 start and 1009–10 consecration, Dakshina Meru, 59.82 m vimana, 13 talas, sanctum
  and linga, circumambulatory path, gopura and court, 81 karanas, living temples.
- **Flagged:** the procession claim for the Cleveland Nataraja itself (InferenceChain → plausible); modern festival
  photos are not evidence of 11th-c practice; ritual varies by temple and sect; “bronze” vs “copper alloy”; the
  departure of many bronzes from temples in the 20th c, some stolen and returned (general, no claim about these objects).
- **Withdrawn while writing:** bathing and feeding of images (not in the sources read); a gopura height comparison.
- **Images:** Cleveland and Walters CC0; Commons — Vengolis (CC BY-SA 4.0), Ms Sarah Welch plan (CC0),
  N. Vivekananthamoorthy (CC BY-SA 4.0), Richard Mortel (CC BY 2.0), Prajnaana (CC BY-SA 4.0), VasuVR (CC BY 4.0).

## Course 34 — Heiji Monogatari Emaki (`emaki-unrolling-story.mdx`, card No.034)

- **ColBase / TNM A-9976:** national treasure; Kamakura 13th c; 42.2 × 952.9 cm; the Heiji disturbance; Nijō's escape in
  a court lady's dress to Rokuhara; four sections; three surviving scrolls (Boston, Seikadō, TNM) and fragments; group
  composition; the courtier/warrior contrast and TNM's tentative reading of the patron; the “trembling brush” attributed
  to Gōzei-in Noriie (1194–1255) and the mid-13th-c dating.
- **Observations made from the scan (stated as such):** text and picture sections alternating; the same cart reappearing;
  carts and figures moving leftward; architecture seen from above with roofs omitted, parallel oblique lines.
- **Flagged:** “direction of movement matches unrolling” as a reading; patronage (InferenceChain → plausible); a changed
  ox colour between scenes noted without explanation.
- **Images:** Google Art Project scan via Commons (PD-Japan / PD-Art). The strip is the whole scroll, black scan bands
  trimmed, resized to 12093 × 520 px; details are crops of the same scan. TNM's own ColBase images were not used.
- **Component:** the Phase 4.11 `ScrollUnroll` was reused unchanged (opens at the right end; arm's-width switch).

## Course 35 — Goryeo celadon (`goryeo-celadon.mdx`, card No.035)

- **Cleveland 1921.634, 1921.631, 1991.58, 1928.173, 1928.171:** dates, sizes, techniques, maebyeong use (wine; recent
  research: sesame oil), kundika in ritual and in aristocratic daily use, about 260 kilns, chrysanthemum tea and wine,
  gold-lacquer repair and early-1900s Japanese collectors, provenance (Ludlow; Severance; Kang Collection).
- **Met Heilbrunn, Soyoung Lee, “Goryeo Celadon” (2003):** colour chemistry and reduction firing (~1150 °C), technology
  adapted from China, Xu Jing's 1123 visit, mid-12th-c turn to native taste, Buan and Gangjin, the sanggam process and
  “a rarity in China”.
- **Primary text:** 徐兢《宣和奉使高麗圖經》卷三十二 陶尊、陶爐 (verbatim from wikisource).
- **Image fidelity:** two official Cleveland photographs of one kundika shown side by side; a large brownish zone on the
  maebyeong shown and left unexplained (cause offered only as hypotheses).
- **Noted inconsistency:** Cleveland's kundika record calls the bird a parrot (title) and a phoenix (description).
- **Images:** Cleveland CC0; the inlay detail is an uncropped-scale crop from the 2552 × 3400 print.

## Course 36 — Angkor and Bagan (`angkor-and-bagan.mdx`, card No.036)

- **UNESCO 668:** 400 km², temples, hydraulic works and roads, successive capitals and social order, Khmer architecture's
  South Asian origin and distinct development, 100,000+ inhabitants, 1907–1992 conservation.
- **Britannica, “Angkor Wat”:** Suryavarman II (1113–c.1150), ~30 years, Vishnu and funerary temple, towers as Meru,
  moat as ocean, 188 m causeway, galleries and reliefs, Jayavarman VII and Buddhism, Theravada monks, 1992/2004.
- **UNESCO 1588:** Bagan period, merit-making, “redistributional Buddhism” and the king as chief donor, river transport,
  3,595 monuments, 13th-c peak, inappropriate 1970s/1990s interventions and earthquake damage.
- **Primary text:** 周達觀《真臘風土記》“官屬”“國主出入” (verbatim from wikisource).
- **Relief identification:** from the photographer's Commons description (south gallery, west wing; parasols, fans,
  fly-whisks), consistent with general references.
- **Flagged:** west orientation (interpretations differ); Zhou Daguan describes Angkor 150 years after Angkor Wat;
  modern visitor routes are not historical ritual routes.
- **Images:** Commons — Steve Jurvetson (CC BY 2.0), Michael Gunther (CC BY-SA 4.0), Jorge Láscar (CC BY 2.0),
  Vyacheslav Argenberg (CC BY 4.0), Jakub Hałun (CC BY-SA 4.0).

## Course 37 — Ife (`ife-heads.mdx`, card No.037)

- **British Museum Af1939,34.1:** full description, leaded zinc-brass, lost-wax, dimensions and weight, 1938 accidental
  find at Wunmonije Compound with sixteen other heads and a half figure, identification and function “remain uncertain”,
  crown and the Ooni, Yoruba oral tradition, Ife's development (c. 800; 1100–1400), comparisons with Greece and Rome and
  the Atlantis suggestion, acquisition (H. M. Bate; Kenneth Clark for the NACF).
- **Kimbell AP 1994.04:** terracotta with red pigment and mica, size, subject matter, the burial-and-ritual account
  (attributed to the museum), findspot types, striations that “may represent” scarification, unparalleled crown, broken
  from a full figure, provenance (Swiss private collection; Entwistle; 1994).
- **Flagged:** “a king” as a reading; the striations; the bias behind the Atlantis idea (attributed to researchers).
  Restitution only mentioned and deferred to the planned Case; Ife kept distinct from Benin (Course 56).
- **Withdrawn while writing:** a thin-wall claim and a head/body proportion claim (not in the sources read).
- **Images:** Commons — sailko (CC BY-SA 3.0), Vassil (CC0), Ukabia (CC BY-SA 3.0, 470 px, not enlarged), FA2010
  (public domain, photographer's release).

# Phase 4.19 — Stage 08 Batch A (courses 74–78): sources and image rights

Checked 2026-10-29.

The Product Owner set a final collection policy for modern and contemporary art:
- Every production lesson awards at least one authentic artwork card.
- When the work cannot lawfully be reproduced, its card is a permanent text-only (typographic) card, and the lesson
  links to the work's official page.
- Copyright decides how a card is presented, not whether the work is collected.

All five principal works in this batch are still in copyright, so all five cards are text-only. None of their images is
reproduced here, nor is any thumbnail, crop or video frame of them. Each lesson teaches its principal work through two
things:
- a verified official page, with a "观看指引" (viewing guide) checklist of what to inspect there;
- lawful supporting images. These are other works, documents, or ArtPilot's own diagrams, each named by its own
  `subject` and never presented as the principal work.

**Operating rules:**
- Image rights are checked in the US and the country of origin; Phases 4.16–4.18 applied the same test.
- A generic Commons tag, a museum's image endpoint, the age of a photograph or an artist's death date is never enough on
  its own.
- When an image is a photograph of a public-domain painting, the photographer's licence is honoured too.
- **CC BY-SA:** the resized file is offered under the same licence.
- **Processing:** images were resized and recompressed only, with no crops or colour changes, unless stated otherwise.

## Earlier bounded search (kept for the record)

The same day, before the text-only policy, a bounded search looked for lawful images of the central works. Its findings
still stand:
- **Museum open access:** Cleveland, AIC, the Met and Smithsonian Open Access have no public-domain images of these
  works.
- **Commons:** files rest on death-date or photographer-licence tags only.
- ***ARTnews*:** renewed issues from 1944 and contributions from 1949, so Namuth's 1951 published photographs of Pollock
  are not public domain.
- **Gutai:** the 1955–56 photographs could not be sourced to an original publication.
- **The 1973 *LOVE* stamp:** the National Postal Museum marks its image "Usage Conditions Apply", and the design's
  history is unresolved.

Under the policy adopted afterwards, these works no longer need images.

## 3. Quotations and translation notes

- **Suger (C-33, C-34).** The Chinese renderings are my translations of Panofsky's English, made for teaching. The manuscript quotes only short phrases, says the Latin was not checked, and does not reproduce Panofsky's English at length. Before publication, check the Latin against Panofsky (1979) or Gasparri's edition (Les Belles Lettres, 1996–2001), and decide whether to quote Latin.
- **Guillaume le Breton, *Philippide* (1214–1217).** S3 quotes a passage praising the new stone vaults as fire-proof. It was not used in the manuscript, to save length, but it is a useful primary witness.
- **Robert of Torigni (1145).** S3 quotes his description of people hauling carts of stone for the earlier façade work (the "cult of the carts"). It was not used: it concerns the 1140s façade, not the post-1194 rebuilding, and is easily misapplied.
- No other direct quotations appear in the manuscript.

## 4. Conflicts recorded

1. **Fire date.** 10 June 1194 (S3) vs "night of 10–11 June" (several secondary sources).
2. **Consecration day.** 17 October 1260 (S3) vs 24 October (secondary sources).
3. **North spire height.** 115 m (S3) vs 113 m (secondary sources). Not used in the manuscript.
4. **Third tier of flying buttresses.** "Later" (S2) vs "14th century" (secondary). The manuscript says "later".
5. **Labyrinth's medieval use.** The rectorate's devotional page (S4) states "les dernières découvertes montrent" an Easter-Vespers purpose. Its research page (S5) shows the evidence is by analogy (Auxerre, Sens) plus Chartres documents that mention dancing but not the labyrinth. The manuscript presents the evidence in layers and does not adopt S4's certainty.
6. **Suger's light theology.** Panofsky vs Kidson and later critics (C-35).
7. **Rib function.** Viollet-le-Duc vs Pol Abraham, and later analysis (C-23).
8. **Trade windows.** Guild donation vs Williams (C-45).
9. **Interior restoration.** Critics vs defenders (C-18).

## 5. Claims removed or kept out

- "Chartres was the first Gothic cathedral" / "Suger invented Gothic". Contradicted by S8, S10 and S17; the manuscript says the opposite.
- "Gothic interiors are bright". Replaced by an evidence-based description (coloured, filtered light; ochre walls).
- "Medieval pilgrims walked the labyrinth on their knees as a substitute for Jerusalem". No medieval evidence found (S5); named as a later claim.
- "The labyrinth centre aligns exactly with the west rose." Repeated on S4, but no medieval textual support (S5); not stated as fact.
- "Stained glass was the Bible of the illiterate." Not presented as a single purpose; replaced by the list of alternatives in S6.
- **Correction pass:** "Pointed arches produce less thrust" (C-21), stated without conditions. Removed.
- **Correction pass:** the paragraph on the origin of the term "Gothic" (C-37). Removed.
- **Correction pass:** "Many earlier cathedrals had four-storey walls" (C-12). Narrowed to what S2 supports.
- **Correction pass:** the restoration start date "2008" and the critics' "weakens the glow" argument (C-18). Removed as unverified.
- Spire heights, exact window counts beyond the official 176, glass area in m², and the number of sculpted figures. Not needed; avoided to reduce unverified figures.
- Named architects. None are known (S2).
- The previous working title's focus on "smallness". Kept only as an opening observation and in the closing paragraph, per the brief.

## Case 03 — Benin plaque 82-5-3 and its return

### Sources used for facts

- Digital Benin, object 82-5-3 (https://digitalbenin.org/catalogue/8253): current institution, Edo name Ama-Ẹrọnmwọn,
  13 iconographic annotations (Odigba, Uhunmwu Ikpotoki, Ẹbẹn, Ebe Amẹ, horn player…), provenance events (production by
  Igun-Ẹrọnmwọn c. 1504–1550; loot 1897–1899; contact with Pitt-Rivers by date of publication and visual
  identification, 1900 catalogue plate 1 fig. 4; purchase 1982; restitution), NMAfA data (dimensions, credit line,
  NMAfA's own dating "mid-16th–17th century"). Pitt-Rivers actor page (Webster as main dealer; Farnham museum dispersed
  early 1960s).
- Smithsonian press release, 11 Oct 2022 (29 of 39 objects; 6 pending; June 2022 vote; ethical returns policy; 20 from
  the Hirshhorn; ceremony attendees; c. 3,000 objects dispersed).
- NMAfA exhibition "Benin Bronzes", from 3 Jun 2024 (nine works on loan, eight in the show, one in the permanent
  galleries; text consulted with Nigerian museum professionals).
- SMB Ethnologisches Museum, "The Benin Collection" (25 Aug 2022 contract; 512 objects; about a third on loan for ten
  years; framing of the invasion; dispersal via Nigerian traders).
- A. Pitt-Rivers, *Antique Works of Art from Benin* (1900), Internet Archive `antiqueworksofar00pittuoft`.
- BBC News, Barnaby Phillips, 10 May 2023 (Buhari declaration of 23 Mar 2023; reactions of the Oba, NCMM, a German
  official; Cambridge postponement; EMOWAA/MOWAA and the planned Benin Royal Museum).
- Museums Journal (Museums Association), 20 Nov 2025 (MOWAA opening disrupted; land licence revoked; presidential
  committee).

### Explicitly not claimed

- Where 82-5-3 is physically now (one of the nine on loan in Washington, or returned): not publicly documented; the
  Case says so and the ProvenanceTrail marks it as a gap.
- Who took it in 1897, and its owners 1900–1982: no record; "probably left Farnham with the early-1960s dispersal" is
  labelled inference.
- No return event, handover or display in Nigeria is invented. Research stops at 30 Oct 2026; the committee's outcome
  was not found.

### Fiction

A composite master and apprentice in a casting workshop (c. 1540) and a composite Washington registrar (2022), each
with a fiction callout. The 1897 section has no fictional characters or dialogue.

## Case 04 — Bonampak Structure 1

### Sources used for facts

- INAH, Bonampak (https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/en/node/4331) and "The Most Significant Places in Bonampak"
  (https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/en/node/5416): building length, rooms numbered 1–3 left to right, lintels (c. 789
  captures; Lintel 2 the Yaxchilán ruler; Lintel 3 some 50 years earlier), niches, room contents, "the heir never
  ruled", Building 6 Lintel 4 (Chan Muwan I c. 600), 1946 Lacandon guides, 1946–48 Carnegie copies by Tejeda and
  Villagra, 1984 restoration, 1990s UNAM photographs, second restoration from 2011.
- Yale News, 15 May 2002 (Mary Miller's Bonampak Documentation Project from 1995: colour transparency, infrared
  photography and video; hand-painted half-size reconstructions from 1999; records presented to Mexico).
- Magaloni et al., *MRS Proceedings* 352 (1995), abstract only (samples dated c. 790–792; organic material in paint
  and mortar; rich palette).
- Valverde Valdés, review of Miller & Brittenham, *The Spectacle of the Late Maya Court*, *Estudios de Cultura Maya*
  XLIII (2014), Redalyc https://www.redalyc.org/articulo.oa?id=281331367008 (100+ figures in Room 2; Yajaw Chan Muwaan
  with 28 others above nine captives; ten lords in two groups before a child in arms; "time doubles"; the eight-level
  pyramid as protagonist; form as political rhetoric; why the ruler is clear only in Room 2).

### Explicitly not claimed

Maya blue chemistry, pigment recipes, underdrawing sequence, unfinished areas, room orientation and the dead captive
are avoided (unverified). The painter's techniques (bark binder, scaffold, enlarged upper figures) are fiction and
flagged in interpretation callouts. Reconstructions are repeatedly marked as not the original.

### Fiction

Composite painter 伊特萨 and assistant 卡维 (invented names; the painters are anonymous). Modern visitor vignette is
labelled imaginative.

### Two separate rights layers

1. **Photographic copyright.** Held by each photographer and licensed as in the table. The murals (c. 790) and the
   building are public domain; INAH's 1984 and 2011 conservation is not treated as new authorship. CC BY / BY-SA
   cover copyright only; Commons' Mexico page deals with copyright and freedom of panorama and says nothing about
   heritage law, so the Commons licence alone does not settle the second layer.
2. **Mexican heritage law (non-copyright).** All seven images show an INAH-administered archaeological monument
   (Bonampak zone, Chiapas).
   - *Ley Federal sobre Monumentos y Zonas Arqueológicos, Artísticos e Históricos*, art. 17 (Cámara de Diputados
     text, read 2026-10-30): reproduction of archaeological monuments **"con fines comerciales"** requires the
     permission of the competent Institute (INAH).
   - Its *Reglamento*, arts. 38–41: a commercial reproduction is a replica "obtenida por cualquier procedimiento o
     medio"; the permit names the approved commercial purpose; authorised reproductions carry "Reproducción
     autorizada por el Instituto competente". INAH applies this to images (procedure INAH-01-004, modality B,
     reproduction of monuments). The procedure page sits behind a bot check and was not read; the law texts were.
   - *Ley Federal de Derechos*, art. 288-D: fees for commercial filming and photography *at* the site, owed by the
     person shooting, not by a later publisher.
   - Nothing in these texts reaches non-commercial reproduction.

**Assessment for ArtPilot's current use.** The site is free, has no ads, payments or sponsorship, and sells nothing.
Under art. 17 its use is not commercial, so no INAH permit is needed, and the seven images are cleared *for the
current use*.

**Condition.** If ArtPilot later charges for access, carries advertising or sponsorship, or uses these images in
marketing or merchandise, all seven become "requires INAH authorization" (or must be replaced) before that change goes
live. This is the same pattern already recorded for Chartres (French *domaines nationaux* rule, Phase 4.0-a).
Course-level precedent: `olmec-head-1.json` already publishes a Commons photo of an INAH-registered monument under the
same reasoning, without a note; a site-wide heritage note is a candidate follow-up, not part of this phase.

## Case 05 — Wayang kulit, Yogyakarta

### Sources used for facts

- UNESCO ICH, Wayang puppet theatre (https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/wayang-puppet-theatre-00063): proclaimed 2003,
  inscribed 2008; dalang, puppets, gamelan; comic characters and social criticism; transmission in families; trend
  towards comic scenes and pop tunes.
- Museum Sonobudoyo, visitor information (20:00–21:30 Ramayana wayang kulit) and "Mengenal Tokoh Pewayangan Sang
  Hanoman" parts 1–2 (Anoman as envoy; mandate to find Sinta, not take her back).
- Britannica, "wayang" (viewing from either side; connoisseurs prefer the shadow side; good right, evil left; all-night
  performances).
- Indonesian Wikipedia: Wayang kulit, Wayang kulit gagrag Yogyakarta, Blencong, Kelir, Cempala, Keprak, Simpingan,
  Pathet, Hanoman, Trijata (materials, joints, horn rods, prada; kelir, blencong, gunungan; simpingan and box sizes;
  keprak in Yogyakarta vs Surakarta style; pathet nem–sanga–manyura; Anoman Duta and Anoman Obong).

### Explicitly not claimed

The troupe and the evening are fiction and explicitly not a portrait of Sonobudoyo's or any real troupe. Hand used for
the cempala, gunungan's role at scene changes, and audience rules are left out. The ShadowStage is labelled a
simplified schematic.

### Audio

None. No rehosted YouTube or other recordings, no synthesised gamelan (test-enforced: no `<audio>`, video hosts,
audio files or `AudioContext`). The prelude sends readers to live or genuine recordings.
